SEC. 01

What must match what

The CPU and the motherboard must share a socket. That single match decides your RAM type, your cooler mount, and half of your shopping list. Pick the CPU first — the board follows, and memory follows the board.

Chipset decoder: B-boards are the sensible middle for most builds. X / Z boards add overclocking and maximum ports. A / H boards are budget — perfectly fine, but glance at VRM cooling and the M.2 count before buying.

    MEMORY, IN THREE RULES

  • R1
    The notch is the key.

    DDR4 and DDR5 have different notch positions and pin counts — a DDR5 stick physically cannot enter a DDR4 board. You don't pick a side; the motherboard already did.

  • R2
    Two sticks beat one.

    2 × 16 GB runs in dual channel — roughly double the memory bandwidth of a single stick. For 32 GB, buy a 2 × 16 kit, never 1 × 32.

  • R3
    Speed needs enabling.

    Kits ship at a safe base speed (e.g. 4800 MT/s). Turn on XMP (Intel) or EXPO (AMD) in the BIOS or you leave performance on the table. ~6000 MT/s is the sweet spot for AM5.

FORM FACTORS

Case rule: an ATX case takes everything. An mATX board fits mATX and ATX cases. An ITX board fits anything — including shoeboxes. An ATX board fits only ATX-capable cases.

01

Prep the bench

Clear a table, get good light, one #2 Phillips. Unbox the motherboard onto its cardboard — never on the antistatic bag, whose inside surface is conductive. Keep screws sorted in a muffin tin or the box lids.

TIPBuild the core (board + CPU + RAM + cooler) on the bench first. Everything is easier before it lives inside a steel box.
02

Seat the CPU

Lift the retention arm, open the plate. Align the gold triangle on the chip with the triangle on the socket and lower it in with zero force — if it needs pushing, it's misaligned. Close plate and arm.

NOTEOn Intel LGA and AMD AM5 the delicate pins live in the socket; on older AM4 they're on the chip. Either way: touch nothing in there.
03

Install memory

Open the end latches, match the stick's notch to the slot's key, press down firmly at both ends until they click. For two sticks, use slots A2 + B2 — usually the 2nd and 4th from the CPU. Your manual confirms.

WARNSticks in the wrong slots can leave the board looking completely dead with everything else perfect.
04

Add the M.2 drive

Slide the drive into the slot at about 30°, press it flat, secure the tiny screw (or tool-less clip). It only fits one way — no force required.

NOTEOn some boards the second M.2 slot steals SATA ports or shares lanes. One glance at the manual saves an hour of debugging.
05

Mount the cooler

If the cooler has a plastic film on the cold plate — peel it now. Place a ~5 mm paste dot at the CPU center; mounting pressure spreads it. Tighten screws gradually in an X pattern, then plug the fan into the header labelled CPU_FAN.

WARNNever power on without the cooler mounted — an uncooled CPU can reach throttling temperatures in seconds.
06

Case: shield + standoffs

Install the I/O shield (if your board uses a separate one) from inside the case. Check the standoffs: exactly one per motherboard hole, and none anywhere else.

WARNThe forgotten I/O shield is the classic first-build tax — discovering it means unplugging the entire finished build.
07

Screw in the board

Lower the board at an angle so the rear ports drop into the shield, set it onto the standoffs, and start every screw before tightening any. Snug is enough — a motherboard is not a wheel lug.

08

Power supply + cables

Mount the PSU, fan toward the vent — fan-down if the case has a bottom vent with clearance, fan-up if it sits on carpet. Connect the 24-pin to the board and the 8-pin EPS to the top-left CPU header. GPU power comes next step.

WARNEPS (CPU) and PCIe (GPU) 8-pin cables look similar but are keyed and wired differently. Never force one into the other's socket.
09

Graphics card

Remove the slot covers, open the latch of the top PCIe x16 slot, press the card in until it clicks, screw the bracket. For cards above ~250 W, run two separate PCIe cables from the PSU rather than one daisy-chained pair.

10

First power-on

Front-panel pins: find PWR_SW in the manual's F_PANEL diagram — it has no polarity. PSU rocker to "I", monitor into the GPU, tap power. Fans spin, screen wakes: you're in the BIOS. Enable XMP/EXPO, then boot your USB installer.

TIPNo POST? Reseat the RAM, recheck the 24-pin and EPS cables, confirm the monitor is in the GPU. That trio fixes most "dead" first boots.
SEC. 03

Power supply

THE PART YOU NEVER CHEAP OUT ON

CALCULATOR
INSIDE →
P-01
Headroom is not waste.

PSUs run most efficiently near 50% load, and modern GPUs spike toward twice their rated board power for milliseconds at a time. The calculator's margin absorbs all of it.

P-02
Efficiency ≠ quality.

"80 Plus" certifies how little power is wasted as heat — nothing more. Judge a unit by platform reputation and warranty: 80+ Gold with 7–10 years of cover is the honest tier to shop in.

P-03
Modularity is comfort, not performance.

Fully modular means only the cables you need and clean airflow. Semi-modular is fine on a budget. Electrically, they're the same idea.

P-04
Match the GPU era.

Cards using the 16-pin 12V-2x6 connector want an ATX 3.x unit with that connector natively — otherwise you're stacking bulky adapters and one more failure point.

P-05
The one hard rule.

Never carry a mystery PSU out of an old prebuilt into a new build. It's the cheapest part in the box — and the only one that can kill everything else on its way out.

SEC. 04

Failure modes

EIGHT CLASSICS — ALL AVOIDABLE, ALL RECOVERABLE

E-01 … E-08
  1. E-01The forgotten I/O shield

    A thin frame behind the motherboard ports, discovered only at step 10 — which means near-full teardown. Fix: it goes in before the board does. Always.

  2. E-02Cooler film left on

    The cold plate ships with a protective peel. Left on, paste never touches metal and the CPU thermal-throttles within minutes. Fix: bare copper, then the paste dot.

  3. E-03RAM in the wrong slots

    Boards POST from specific slots first; sticks in slots 1+2 can look like a dead board. Fix: A2 + B2 — second and fourth from the CPU, per the manual.

  4. E-04EPS / PCIe cable swap

    Both are 8-pin, both click; one in the wrong socket won't boot, and forcing it can fry a board. Fix: the cable marked CPU/EPS goes to the board's top-left; PCIe/VGA goes to the GPU.

  5. E-05Monitor in the motherboard

    With a discrete GPU installed, the board's own video out is usually disabled: fans spin, screen stays black, panic begins. Fix: display cable into the graphics card.

  6. E-06Daisy-chained GPU power

    One cable with two pigtails can't safely feed a hungry card through transient spikes — random shutdowns under load. Fix: two separate PCIe cables above ~250 W.

  7. E-07The stray standoff

    An extra brass pillar behind the board, quietly shorting traces: a "dead" board that was assembled perfectly. Fix: one standoff per hole, zero extras.

  8. E-08Front-panel pin roulette

    Power button wired to the wrong header pair — the PC is fine, the switch isn't. Fix: the manual's F_PANEL diagram; PWR_SW has no polarity, so +/− doesn't matter.

Three checks, in order: (1) length — the case lists a max GPU length, compare it to the card; (2) width — 2, 2.5, 3 or 3.5-slot cards need the corresponding clearance; (3) power — the PSU needs the right connector and enough watts (see the calculator above).
